In the labour market domain, various taxonomies exist for different types of concepts, such as occupations, skills, or working equipment.

A taxonomy typically organises concepts into a hierarchical structure. Taxonomies are primarily used as classification systems.

An ontology, in contrast, provides a richer representation of a domain by defining not only classes, but also the properties, relations, and constraints connecting them. Ontologies aim to represent domain knowledge and often integrate information from multiple sources. In this way, they can serve as knowledge bases that support further analysis and reasoning.

Ontologies can establish connections between different taxonomies. The GLMO integrates separate taxonomies for labor market entities, such as occupations or skills, into a common ontology.
